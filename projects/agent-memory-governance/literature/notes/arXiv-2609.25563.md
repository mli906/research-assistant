# AkasicMEM: governed enterprise memory for agents (arXiv:2609.25563v1, cs.DB, 2026-09-22)
Read: full text (arXiv PDF, 9 pp, reviewer, 2026-09-25). Tags: mechanisms, threats, gaps-check.

- **Claim.** It defines "authorization continuity": source restrictions keep applying through source→memory and memory→memory derivation and through changes of principal or policy. Effective authorization is A_t(m) = R_t(m) ∩ ⋂_{x∈anc_t(m)} R_t(x), re-evaluated at retrieval against the *current* authorization graph. There is an explicit, recorded declassification exception, gated by an NLI test or a human reviewer. Forgetting removes content and embeddings but keeps the vertex in the lineage graph. It is built on GraphAI's AkasicDB (a unified vector–graph–relational DBMS), so lineage traversal and policy evaluation run in one query plan.
- **Evaluation.** **None.** This is a system/vision paper.
- **Discussion items that overlap our gaps (read closely).**
  - §5.1: derived memory "should inherit the most restrictive" retention period of its sources. Deletion through lineage is many-to-one, and "deletion must be verifiable… probing for memory semantically close to the deleted content". Policy must be versioned to reproduce past decisions. (Overlaps GP1, GP4, GP6.)
  - §5.2: the automated declassification test is "necessary but not sufficient, because a text may be independent of a source sentence by sentence yet disclose it **in aggregate**". (This names GP2's composition problem.)
  - §5.3 sets out **an evaluation plan that is nearly identical to our proposed benchmark**: "a synthetic organization with sources, principals, and authority relationships, a task stream that forms and derives memory, and a timeline of governance events such as revocations, corrections, and deletions"; leakage against derivation depth and time since policy change; over-restriction; overhead; adversarial reconstruction probes; comparison against ungoverned, scope-isolated and governed-shared baselines.
- **Relevance.** It does not empirically pre-empt anything. But it announces a competing evaluation from a DB group with its own system, and it pre-states most of our design choices, which lowers the novelty of the benchmark *design*. The finance obligations (record classes, barriers with Brewer–Nash history, jurisdictional retention) are absent.
