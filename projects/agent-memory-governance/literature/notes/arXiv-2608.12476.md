# Governed Persistent Memory (GPM): source-bound state semantics and fail-closed release (arXiv:2608.12476v2, 2026-08-26)
Read: full text (arXiv PDF v2, 23 pp, extracted with pypdf and read in full by the reviewer, 2026-09-25). Tags: gaps-check.

- **Problem.** Retrieval relevance is not "public assertability". Contradicted, superseded, retracted, deleted or stale records must not support an outgoing claim.
- **Mechanism.** A hash-chained, bitemporal event ledger (episode.add, fact.assert, fact.quarantine, fact.retract, user.delete). Lifecycle state is *derived* from events. A public projection V_pub applies conflict isolation, supersession, retraction and the latest per-user deletion barrier at the *current* verified head, even for historical reads ("non-revival"). There is a **separately routed audit view** (`auditView`/`auditSearch`) that reconstructs as-of state, deleted and retracted items included. A structured-claim release gate (I5) checks exact claim closure against a fresh view with a stable head. Five executable clauses I1–I5.
- **Evaluation.** (1) GPM-ReleaseBench v1: 12 synthetic categories (incl. cross-user mixing, retraction non-revival, deletion non-revival, deletion-index integrity), 3,600 evaluator-hidden cases. GPM matches 3,600/3,600; the best simple policy matches 1,800 and releases unsafely on 50% of violation cases. The benchmark was designed by the same project; there was no human annotation. (2) A sealed end-to-end service test: 2,400/2,400 vs 600/2,400 for an ungoverned local Qwen2.5-7B. (3) A bounded finite model (331,776 states) and a 100k-trace three-engine differential. (4) Clean-task controls: LongMemEval judge gate failed (kappa < 0.8), so it reports no answer-accuracy claim.
- **What it does NOT cover (stated).** "Append-only audit and deletion rights remain in tension… Our result is a public-view barrier plus a separately routed audit view. It is **not media erasure, backup deletion, cryptographic erasure, or machine unlearning**. Production deployments still require retention policy…" It has no retention periods, no expiry deletion, no legal hold, no record classes and no must-retain scoring. Deletion is per-user (all history), not per-item under a record class. Derived artifacts (summaries, embeddings, skills) are out of scope for I4 (it points to MemoRepair). Single author at a company with commercial interest; it runs a Qwen 7B baseline only; the audit-view separation "is not itself authentication or authorization".
- **Relevance to our gaps.** GP1: it pre-empts the *mechanism pattern* "restrict from operational recall, keep for audit via a second access path" and gives a deterministic non-revival test. It does **not** pre-empt a benchmark that jointly scores mandated retention, erasure and expiry. GP3: it covers source binding and authority-by-provenance for a single user. There are no scopes or principals beyond per-user, so it is not multi-principal. GP4: explicitly out of scope. GP7: its three-engine differential is a precedent for "same contract, several implementations", but only within its own engines.
- **New neighbours cited.** StateFuse (arXiv:2607.05844), TOKI (arXiv:2606.06240), MemIR (arXiv:2605.25869), MemoRepair (arXiv:2605.07242).
