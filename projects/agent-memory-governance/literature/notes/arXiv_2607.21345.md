# Regulating autonomous and agentic AI (arXiv:2607.21345), Reed et al. 2026
Read: abstract only (2026-09-24). Facet: regulation.
- Claim: regulatory assumptions that the regulatee knows about and controls its tools break down for agentic AI; responsibility is spread across the AI supply chain. Retrospective supervisory oversight "becomes ineffective as a risk management tool". Compares UK content platforms, data protection, UK financial services and the EU AI Act, and argues for moving regulation from reactive to active.
- Relevance: supports a design stance in which agent memory carries *ex ante*, machine-checkable policy (retention class, purpose, barrier label) rather than relying on after-the-fact log review.
- Critique: abstract-level only; how it treats record-keeping and memory is unknown. Read the full text before citing specifics.
