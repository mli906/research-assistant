## 1. Ten findings that should shape the design
1. **World-model diagnostics are converging on "does the latent rank decisions correctly", not "can I decode the state".** Linear-probe R^2 stayed within 0.03 across LeWM variants whose online success differed by up to 43 percentage points (`arXiv:2608.18746` [FT]); rank correlation between latent cost and real cost (Plan-Real Spearman) separates them. H that this holds in those four robotics tasks, M that it transfers (speculation: finance has no simulator-based "real cost").
2. **Counterfactual validity needs paired ground truth.** Best recipe in the library: Do-JEPA's paired roll-outs from one restored simulator state with hidden confounders (`arXiv:2609.37378` [FT]); CoPhy (`arXiv:1909.12000` [A]) and CausalProfiler (`arXiv:2511.22842` [A]) give the same logic for synthetic SCMs. Finance has no restorable simulator, so counterfactual claims can only be validated (a) in synthetic economies with known mechanism and (b) against quasi-experiments such as identified monetary shocks (`doi:10.1257/0002828042002651`, `doi:10.1257/mac.20130329`; see `doi:10.1257/jep.32.3.59` for identification limits). H.
3. **Leakage in finance has at least eight distinct channels, and the foundation-model era added two** (parameter look-ahead; cross-series temporal overlap). Section 3 table. The best single piece of evidence is the one-switch benchmark: centred rolling features or full-bar execution inflate Sharpe by ~4.3-29.8 units while other "classic" leaks (global normalisation, same-day close) were ~0 on average (`arXiv:2605.23959` [FT]). H on the direction, M on magnitude (oracle-like protocols).
4. **Training on future data can hurt return forecasts** (negative in 18 of 20 model-horizon cells, median -5.94 pp vs historical mean) (`arXiv:2609.20554` [FT]). So contamination is not always inflationary in the low-SNR regime; but it still voids the point-in-time claim. M.
5. **Generic TS leaderboards do not rank finance models.** FinVerse vs GIFT-Eval rank correlation r = 0.40 (`arXiv:2608.03259` [FT]; `arXiv:2410.10393` [A]); FinStressTS: AR/HAR/DLinear beat Transformers in point forecasts, neural models need 2-3x more data for calibration parity (`arXiv:2606.03184` [FT]). M-H.
6. **Independent evidence for finance TSFMs is weak**: gains over random walk "small and sparse" (`arXiv:2606.27100` [FT]); only Tiny Time Mixers beats Log-HAR for realised volatility, narrowly (`arXiv:2607.05291` [A]); a critical 2026 review finds "no general AI architecture is shown to deliver persistent, cross-regime, capacity-aware net alpha" (`arXiv:2609.04917` [A]). Vendor claims are self-benchmarked (see industry.md). H.
7. **A vintage-consistent macro protocol exists and is cheap**: MACROCAST evaluates 123 FRED-MD series, h = 0..12, monthly ALFRED vintages, "6-month delayed actuals", RMSFE vs AR(1) (`arXiv:2606.28670` [FT]). Pooled ratio 0.971 at h=1 (0.995 at h=12): the headroom in macro nowcasting is a few percent outside labour-market series. H on the protocol, M on the numbers.
8. **Multiplicity is the main statistical threat to a paper that tries many variants.** t > 3.0 hurdle (`doi:10.1093/rfs/hhv059` [A]), probability of backtest overfitting (`doi:10.21314/jcf.2016.322` [A]), deflated Sharpe (`doi:10.3905/jpm.2014.40.5.094`), 65% of 452 anomalies fail t = 1.96 after microcap mitigation (`doi:10.1093/rfs/hhy131` [A]), but the opposite view: most factors replicate in a Bayesian model across 93 countries (`doi:10.1111/jofi.13249` [A]). The literature disagrees; pre-register and count trials. H.
9. **Data scarcity is real at the entity level, not the market level.** Public firm panels are ~10^3-10^4 entities x ~10^2 quarters (table below); macro panels are ~10^2 series x ~10^3 months with vintages; private firms have essentially no free financials. Mitigation seen in the 2026 literature: pretrain on synthetic data then fine-tune on real vintages (`arXiv:2606.28670`) or synthetic only (EXAONE Finance, `arXiv:2609.04239` [FT]). M.
10. **Decision-aligned and calibration metrics are under-used**: point accuracy does not guarantee calibration (TimesFM, Moirai overconfident at long horizons; Chronos-2 best balance) on SPY and two non-finance series (`arXiv:2609.25788` [FT]); proper scoring rules and calibration theory are old and sufficient (`doi:10.1198/016214506000001437`, `doi:10.1111/j.1467-9868.2007.00587.x`); conformal methods for shift exist (`arXiv:2106.00170`, `arXiv:2010.09107`). M-H.

## 2. How world models and latent representations are evaluated
### 2.1 Taxonomy
| Layer | What it tests | Representative evidence (id) | Fails when | Finance analogue |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Representation health without labels | Collapse / effective rank / information content of embeddings | RankMe `arXiv:2210.02885` [A], LiDAR `arXiv:2312.04000` [A] | Rank is high but uninformative | Monitor during pretraining; cheap, label-free |
| Probing (decodability) | Is variable X linearly/shallowly readable from the latent | linear probes `arXiv:1610.01644` [A]; control tasks `arXiv:1909.03368` [A]; criteria for "has a world model" incl. non-triviality conditions `arXiv:2507.21513` [A] | Decodable but not used for prediction/decisions (`arXiv:2608.18746`) | Probe recession state, leverage, sector; require control tasks (shuffled-date labels) |
| Latent roll-out error | Multi-step error in embedding space | V-JEPA 2-AC and LeWM line (`arXiv:2506.09985`); VICReg-style latents give distances that are not comparable across models | Latent scale arbitrary; error small because representation collapsed to slow features | Report **both** latent and decoded observation-space error; normalise by persistence baseline (MASE-like) |
| Observation-space roll-out / forecasting | Decode and compare with realised data | TS benchmarks: `arXiv:2509.26468` fev-bench [A], `arXiv:2410.10393` GIFT-Eval [A], `arXiv:2403.20150` TFB [A] | Metrics on raw levels reward persistence | Skill vs random walk/AR(1)/HAR; Diebold-Mariano `doi:10.1080/07350015.1995.10524599`; Giacomini-Rossi for unstable environments `doi:10.1002/jae.1177` |
| Planning / decision metrics | Do plans chosen in latent space succeed | DINO-WM `arXiv:2411.04983`, PLDM `arXiv:2502.14819`; Plan-Real Spearman `arXiv:2608.18746`; stage-wise reliability `arXiv:2609.07126` [A/FT] | Latent distance not aligned with real cost | Rank scenarios/policies by realised loss; portfolio/hedge outcome (but see leakage) |
| Robustness / sensitivity | Rollout invariance to nuisance perturbation, separation of true state differences | ACPC IR/SR `arXiv:2608.12939` [FT] | Needs a reference checkpoint; tested only on blur/noise | Perturb reporting lags, vendor definitions, restatement noise |
| Counterfactual / interventional | Predicted effect of action a vs reference a0 | Do-JEPA `arXiv:2609.37378` [FT]; Causal-JEPA `arXiv:2602.11389` [A]; theory `arXiv:2609.31161` (identifiability needs diverse action-induced changes); DoFlow `arXiv:2511.02137` [A] | Hidden confounding of actions; no restorable simulator | Synthetic SCM economies; identified shocks as external check (Section 3.6) |
| Model-recovery / coherence | Does the model recover the true generating structure | Myhill-Nerode world-model metrics `arXiv:2406.03689` [A]: models pass standard diagnostics yet are "far less coherent than they appear" | Only for deterministic automata | Known-mechanism synthetic markets (FinStressTS) |
| Open-ended task batteries | Reward-free exploration then derived tests | WorldTest/AutumnBench `arXiv:2510.19788` [A] (43 environments, 129 tasks, 517 humans; humans beat frontier models) | Games only | Multi-task battery per use case |
| Calibration / uncertainty | Coverage and sharpness of predictive distributions | Gneiting scoring rules; `arXiv:1706.04599` [A]; `arXiv:2609.25788` [FT]; adaptive conformal `arXiv:2106.00170` | Point-accurate yet overconfident at long horizons | CRPS + coverage at 80/90%; conformal wrappers for shifted regimes |
| Theory-linked diagnostics | Bounds connecting latent quality to planning regret | `arXiv:2606.27014` (JEPA risk to regret, from scan); ACPC Props 1-2 | Deterministic-dynamics assumption | Use as sanity check, not as evidence |

### 2.2 What changed in the last 12 months (M)
- A cluster of 2026 diagnostics papers built on the LeWM/SIGReg line: ACPC (`arXiv:2608.12939`), decision-metric alignment (`arXiv:2608.18746`), stage-wise reliability (`arXiv:2609.07126`), temporal-distance plan-aware JEPA (`arXiv:2607.25337` [A]), latent-geometry amortised planning (`arXiv:2605.08732` [A]), "latent state design under sufficiency constraints" taxonomy (`arXiv:2605.01694` [A]: latent state for prediction, control, planning, memory, grounding or counterfactual reasoning, each with different sufficiency). One diagnostic paper, VIScore (arXiv:2608.11174), was **withdrawn from arXiv for licensing reasons**; I did not rely on it (tag `withdrawn`).
- Counterfactual evaluation moved from video-physics benchmarks (CoPhy 2019) to paired latent-effect training and testing (Do-JEPA, Sept 2026).
- Everything above is in robotics/control simulators with 4 short-horizon tasks, single runs, and Euclidean latent costs (limitations stated in `arXiv:2608.18746`, `arXiv:2608.12939`). No finance transfer exists (H, from searches).

### 2.3 Open problems the authors state
Generalisation to partial observability, larger models, other planners (`arXiv:2608.18746`); no unaugmented reference checkpoint = no IR-SR screen (`arXiv:2608.12939`); exact paired interventions need a restorable simulator, object-aligned slots and a single direct target per action (`arXiv:2609.37378`); magnitude of temporal leakage in real TSFM settings "largely unknown" (`arXiv:2510.13654`).

## 3. Finance forecasting and representation benchmarks, and their pitfalls
### 3.1 Leakage and bias channels (checklist for our harness)
| # | Channel | Evidence | Control |
|---|---|---|---|
| L1 | Feature look-ahead (centred windows, future-informed graphs) | LG-Sharpe up to ~25 for centred features (`arXiv:2605.23959`) | Trailing windows only; unit tests that inject each leak |
| L2 | Normalisation fitted on full sample | ~0 average effect in `arXiv:2605.23959` but classic risk | Fit on train window only |
| L3 | Execution timing (close vs next open) | LG-Sharpe up to ~29.8 (`arXiv:2605.23959`) | t+1 open labels |
| L4 | Survivorship / universe selection | Banz-Breen: Compustat look-ahead and ex-post-selection biases change portfolio returns and conclusions (`doi:10.1111/j.1540-6261.1986.tb04548.x` [A]); `arXiv:2605.23959` does *not* model PIT universes; FinVerse timestamps some indicators at measurement not release (`arXiv:2608.03259`) | Index-membership as of date; CRSP delisting returns |
| L5 | Restatement / data-vintage leakage | Real-time data sets for macro (`doi:10.1016/s0304-4076(01)00072-0`, `doi:10.1016/s1574-0706(05)01017-7`); MACROCAST names "revision bias" (`arXiv:2606.28670`); SEC FSDS are "as filed" (section 4); AuditFraudBench builds on original vs restated 10-K/10-Q (`arXiv:2606.08345` [A]) | ALFRED vintages; EDGAR as-filed with filing dates; Compustat Snapshot |
| L6 | Publication/reporting lag | MacroLens: fundamentals lag 1-90 days (`arXiv:2606.24950` [A]); delay-robust modelling (`arXiv:2603.20869` [A]) | Gate every input by release timestamp |
| L7 | Pretraining contamination (data) | Only 6% of 401 datasets were never in any TSFM's pretraining; contamination effects >50% in cases; TSFMAudit detects it via probe-adaptation dynamics on 187 datasets/6 TSFMs (`arXiv:2510.13654`, `arXiv:2605.26161` [A]) | New data after all cutoffs; audit |
| L8 | Pretraining contamination (parameters) and cross-series temporal overlap | Look-ahead in weights (`arXiv:2609.20554`); DAX-trained model gained ~37% on S&P 500 over the same crash (`arXiv:2510.13654`) | Annual-vintage pretraining or global cutoff before test; purge *all* series, not just the target |
| L9 | LLM memorisation | Look-ahead and "distraction" bias in LLM sentiment trading (`arXiv:2309.17322` [A]); `doi:10.2139/ssrn.4754678` (Lookahead bias in pretrained LMs, no abstract retrieved); Look-Ahead-Bench (`arXiv:2601.13770` [A]); LiveMacroEval hourly live nowcasts to escape memorisation (`arXiv:2608.30110` [A]) | Relevant to the later filings/text extension, not tabular v1 |
| L10 | Selection / multiplicity | See Section 3.3 | Trial ledger, deflated metrics, pre-registration |
| L11 | Cross-validation misuse with temporal dependence | `doi:10.1016/j.ins.2011.12.028` [A]; `doi:10.1016/j.csda.2017.11.003` [A]: K-fold CV is valid for pure autoregression with uncorrelated errors but not generally; `doi:10.1145/2382577.2382579` leakage in data mining [A]; common pitfalls `arXiv:2203.10716` [A] | Rolling-origin; embargo/purge |

### 3.3 Backtest overfitting, factor zoo, replication
- Selection bias and multiplicity: `doi:10.1090/noti1105` (Pseudo-mathematics), `doi:10.21314/jcf.2016.322` (PBO via combinatorially symmetric CV), `doi:10.3905/jpm.2014.40.5.094` (deflated Sharpe), `doi:10.3905/jpm.2015.42.1.013` (Backtesting), and the older data-snooping line `doi:10.1093/rfs/3.3.431`. ML-era comparison of out-of-sample testing methods: `doi:10.1016/j.knosys.2024.112477` [A]. All [A] except where noted; H on content (well-established).
- The factor zoo: `doi:10.1093/rfs/hhv059`; "Discount rates" presidential address as origin of the term `doi:10.1111/j.1540-6261.2011.01671.x`; `doi:10.1093/rfs/hhy131`; counter-view `doi:10.1111/jofi.13249`; p-hacking limits `doi:10.1111/jofi.13036` (published t-stats above 6 imply astronomical p-hacking if all spurious); specification errors beyond p-hacking ("factor mirage") `doi:10.3905/jpm.2025.1.794`.
- ML for returns: `doi:10.1093/rfs/hhaa009` (trees and NNs best; dominant signals momentum, liquidity, volatility), `doi:10.1111/jofi.13298` (virtue of complexity), `doi:10.1016/j.jeconom.2020.07.009` (autoencoder asset pricing), `doi:10.1146/annurev-financial-101521-104735` (review), and the sceptical Goyal-Welch result that many predictors fail out of sample (`doi:10.1093/rfs/hhm014`). `arXiv:2512.16251` [A] offers a concept-bottleneck pricing model (tabular, not world model).
- Take-away for design: evaluation on **return prediction is the worst-case test bed** for demonstrating a world model (SNR ~1% R^2 ceiling quoted in `arXiv:2606.27100`), and the PI's brief already scopes alpha out. Use returns only as a probe with deflated metrics.

### 3.4 Regime shift and non-stationarity
- Structural-break aware comparison: `doi:10.1002/jae.1177`; inflation forecastability shifted (`doi:10.1111/j.1538-4616.2007.00014.x`); stylised facts that any synthetic generator must reproduce (`doi:10.1080/713665670`).
- 2025-26: test-time adaptation from synthetic regime shifts to finance (`arXiv:2602.00073` [A]); TSFMs recover faster than classical models after shocks but still degrade (`arXiv:2506.15705` [A]); macro double-descent/benign-overfitting with synthetic augmentation (`arXiv:2605.15358` [A]); regimes in HEPA (scan).
- Protocol element: report performance **by regime and by test year** (e.g. as `arXiv:2605.23959` does: 8-9 of 9 years), exclude or flag COVID quarters explicitly (MACROCAST excluded Q1-Q2 2020; that choice must be pre-registered, not post hoc).

### 3.5 Synthetic benchmarks with known mechanism
FinStressTS (`arXiv:2606.03184`); synthetic financial data generators (`arXiv:2512.21791` [A], Fiaingen `arXiv:2510.01169` [A]); EXAONE and MACROCAST use synthetic pretraining; scenario generation comparison (`arXiv:2606.11859`, from scan). Missing: a synthetic economy with **actions, confounding and latent firm states** to test counterfactual validity; this is a candidate contribution (speculation, M that none exists, from searches).

### 3.6 Actions/shocks as quasi-ground-truth for counterfactual tests
Identified monetary shocks: narrative (`doi:10.1257/0002828042002651`), high-frequency surprises (`doi:10.1257/mac.20130329`), VAR critique (`doi:10.2307/1912017`), identification overview (`doi:10.1257/jep.32.3.59`). Natural-disaster shocks to supply chains: `doi:10.1093/qje/qjw018` (affected suppliers impose output losses on customers; effect larger for specific inputs) and `doi:10.1093/qje/qjaa044` (Great East Japan Earthquake: -0.47 pp Japanese GDP growth); network structure `doi:10.3982/ecta9623`; return predictability from links `doi:10.1111/j.1540-6261.2008.01379.x`. These give **published effect sizes to compare a world model's counterfactual against**, with the caveat that each is a different identification design (M).

Scale conclusion (M): a firm-level world model trained on public US data sees on the order of 10^3-10^4 firms x ~70 quarterly XBRL observations (2009+) or ~10^2 quarters in Compustat; the macro layer has ~10^2 series x ~800 months with ~25 years of vintages. That is **orders of magnitude below video JEPA pretraining** (1M+ hours, `arXiv:2506.09985`) and below TSFM corpora (~10^11 points). Hence the frequent move to synthetic pretraining and to small models (MACROCAST 1M parameters; EXAONE 202M).

## 5. Recommended evaluation protocol elements (for experiments/plan.md; proposals, not yet pre-registered)
1. **Three-layer evaluation per use case** (representation / dynamics / use), each with pre-declared primary and secondary metrics: (a) label-free health (RankMe, LiDAR) plus probes with control tasks; (b) latent and decoded multi-step error, normalised by persistence and AR/HAR/DFM baselines, with Diebold-Mariano and Giacomini-Rossi tests; (c) decision metric aligned to the use (scenario ranking by realised loss, nowcast skill, network-shock RMSE).
2. **Vintage-consistent real-time harness** modelled on MACROCAST (ALFRED vintages; delayed actuals; expanding windows) for macro; EDGAR as-filed with filing timestamps for fundamentals; gate every input by release time (L5, L6).
3. **Leak unit tests**: inject each leak L1-L3 (and L5, L8) into a synthetic panel and require the harness to detect it, following the one-switch design (`arXiv:2605.23959`). Avoid centred context-target windows when targets overlap context in time.
4. **Contamination policy for any pretrained comparator**: test window starts after every comparator's and our own pretraining cutoff, purging all series sharing dates (`arXiv:2510.13654`), or retrain annual vintages (`arXiv:2609.20554`) for the small models we control; run a TSFMAudit-style check for released TSFM baselines.
5. **Baselines that are hard to beat**: random walk, AR(1), HAR/Log-HAR (volatility), DFM and BVAR (macro), gradient-boosted trees on lagged features (earnings, `doi:10.1111/1475-679x.12429`), TSFMs (Chronos-2, TimesFM, Moirai-2, TTM, Kronos/FinCast/EXAONE where weights are available; EXAONE's licence is non-commercial, non-derivative).
6. **Synthetic known-mechanism arena for counterfactuals**: extend FinStressTS-style generators with latent state, exogenous action, and hidden confounder; evaluate paired effect error (Do-JEPA recipe), identification failure modes (action diversity, `arXiv:2609.31161`), and report where the model *cannot* be identified.
7. **Real-world counterfactual sanity checks**: compare predicted response to identified shocks (monetary surprises, disasters) against published effect sizes; report as exploratory, not as validation (selection of shocks is a researcher degree of freedom; pre-register the shock list).
8. **Calibration**: CRPS/pinball loss, interval coverage at 80/90%, reliability diagrams; conformal wrapper under shift (`arXiv:2106.00170`). Report calibration separately by regime.
9. **Regime and subsample reporting**: by test year, by NBER recession/expansion, COVID inclusion/exclusion pre-declared; heterogeneity across entities (small vs large firms), because averages hide outlier-driven wins (`arXiv:2607.05291`).
10. **Multiplicity discipline**: log every configuration tried (trial ledger), report deflated metrics when selecting among variants (`doi:10.3905/jpm.2014.40.5.094`, `doi:10.21314/jcf.2016.322`), and use `tools/seal.py` held-out for the final comparison.
11. **Any trading probe**: only after the above, with costs, capacity and PIT universe caveats; report as non-headline (matches the brief).
12. **Compare, don't only absolute-score**: include FinVerse-style ranking with `arXiv:2608.03259` as an arena for baselines, noting its release-date caveat, and, if licences allow, the JKP Common Task Framework.

## 6. Contradictions with what the PI (and brief) already believes
- The brief frames JEPA's non-generative nature as an issue for scenario *generation*; the evaluation literature adds a second issue: **the evaluation of a latent model's scenarios has no accepted metric** in finance. The only institutional example of conditional macro "scenarios" from a foundation model is BISTRO (industry.md), reported without quantitative evaluation in what I could retrieve.
- "Leakage inflates results" is not always true: future data worsened return forecasts in `arXiv:2609.20554`. Expect null results for returns and report them (CLAUDE.md integrity rule).
- Strong generic time-series benchmark ranks do not transfer to finance (r = 0.40), so published TSFM leaderboards are weak priors for our baselines.
