## 1. Headline findings (for the PI)
1. **Finance-JEPA is no longer empty ground.** An ICML 2026 poster (Mahns, Balestriero, Assran: "Joint-Embedding Predictive Learning of Latent Market States in U.S. Equities", web, https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/poster/65643, abstract only, accessed 2026-10-01) trains a permutation-invariant tokenizer plus temporal JEPA on daily cross-sections of US equities; the embedding tracks second-moment structure (realised vol, correlation concentration, effective factor dimension), only weakly direction, predicts gradual recovery but not sudden stress. Also a single-author Fin-JEPA (`doi:10.2139/ssrn.6855118`, code README (web): 367K params, 6,230 equities, +10.1% over identity baseline, "weak-to-negligible" trading signal) and an agent-generated, unevaluated action-conditioned "FinJEPA" on Hugging Face (web). See `notes/doi-10.2139-ssrn.6855118.md`. This corrects the early-scan item 1 in STATE.md. Open space remains for firm-level panels, partial observability, action conditioning with an identification argument, and honest comparison to TSFMs/tabular FMs/econometrics. Confidence: high that these exist, low on their quality (not read in full).
2. **Return forecasting is the wrong headline.** Pretrained TSFMs zero-shot are at or below zero R2_OOS on daily returns (`doi:10.2139/ssrn.5770562`: Chronos-large R2_OOS -1.37%, TimesFM-500M -2.80%, CatBoost -0.10%), finance-native pretraining only reaches CatBoost-level directional accuracy (51.7% vs 51.2%), and the June 2026 five-stock study finds "small and sparse" gains (`arXiv:2606.27100`). Kronos (`doi:10.1609/aaai.v40i30.39730`) reports large relative RankIC gains from finance-native tokenised pretraining but on tiny absolute RankICs. A world model should be judged on second-moment and state structure (vol, correlation, regimes), consistent with the ICML JEPA finding.
3. **Most tractable use case given data scarcity: (b) firm-fundamentals nowcasting/imputation with a latent state**, followed by (a) macro scenarios via identified shocks; (c) supply chain is the least tractable on public data (section 7). Counterfactual firm "what-ifs" are not identifiable from observational firm panels without extra structure (section 5). Confidence: medium.
4. **Missing-data baseline warning.** `arXiv:2207.13071` (abs): simple cross-sectional-mean imputation is competitive with EM because missingness is blockwise, predictors weakly correlated, and organised by data source; `doi:10.1093/rfs/hhae036` (abs) proposes a structured imputer and shows 70%+ of firms are affected. A latent-state model has to beat both, not zero-fill.
5. **Counterfactual claims have a precise econometric bar** (invertibility/information set, instrument validity, policy invariance), summarised in section 5; a rich latent state helps exactly where small VARs fail (`arXiv:2307.06145`).

## 3. Pillar 2: partial observability and nowcasting

### 3.1 Dynamic factor models (DFM) and deep DFMs
Foundations: Giannone-Reichlin-Small nowcasting `doi:10.1016/j.jmoneco.2008.05.010`; Stock-Watson principal components `doi:10.1198/016214502388618960`; Doz-Giannone-Reichlin two-step Kalman `doi:10.1016/j.jeconom.2011.02.012`; Banbura-Modugno ML estimation with arbitrary missing patterns `doi:10.1002/jae.2306`; review `doi:10.1146/annurev-economics-080217-053214`. Deep versions: D2FM `arXiv:2007.11887` (abs; beats a state-of-the-art DFM in a real-time US exercise, handles mixed frequency and asynchronous releases), NCDENow (DFM + neural CDE) `arXiv:2409.08732` (abs; South Korea, UK GDP, 6 baselines), Transformer dynamic factors with a conventional factor-model prior `arXiv:2601.12039` (abs: prior improves small-sample performance). ML nowcasting: `doi:10.1016/j.ijforecast.2020.10.005`, LSTM nowcasting `arXiv:2106.08901`, ML regressions `arXiv:2005.14057` (all abs). Operational standards: Atlanta Fed GDPNow (bridge equations + DFM for filling missing monthly data) and NY Fed Staff Nowcast (DFM) (web, https://www.atlantafed.org/research-and-data/data/gdpnow/explainer, https://www.newyorkfed.org/research/policy/nowcast/faqs.html, accessed 2026-10-01).
Gap: no JEPA or masked-latent-prediction nowcaster evaluated against DFM with ragged edges that I could find (confidence medium, search incomplete). Note the Transformer-DFM paper's finding that a conventional factor prior helps in small data supports "structured prior + learned latent" designs.

## 5. Pillar 3/4: counterfactual and causal use: what econometrics says
1. **Shock identification is established but contested.** High-frequency identification (HFI) uses narrow-window futures surprises as instruments: Gertler-Karadi `doi:10.1257/mac.20130329`, Nakamura-Steinsson `doi:10.1093/qje/qjy004` (information effect: surprises also reveal the central bank's view of the economy), Jarocinski-Karadi `doi:10.1257/mac.20180090`, Bauer-Swanson `doi:10.1086/723574`, Miranda-Agrippino-Ricco `doi:10.1257/mac.20180124`, Romer-Romer `doi:10.1257/0002828042002651`, Gurkaynak-Sack-Swanson `doi:10.17016/feds.2004.66`, Stock-Watson external instruments `doi:10.1111/ecoj.12593`. Instrument validity is questioned in an SVAR-with-heteroskedasticity test (SciSpace hit, Quantitative Economics 2023, `doi:10.3982/qe1511`, not stored; abstract says HFI instruments "show signs of invalidity").
2. **Small VARs fail on information.** `arXiv:2307.06145` (note): Proxy VAR cannot recover a non-invertible shock even without noise; Proxy DFM can; monetary tightening effects in FRED-MD puzzle-free. Single paper, simulation plus one application: confidence medium.
3. **What is identified: impulse responses to identified shocks.** Local projections `doi:10.1257/0002828053828518` and VARs estimate the same responses in population `doi:10.3982/ecta17813`; LPs vs VARs bias-variance `arXiv:2104.00655`; inference `arXiv:2007.13888`; structural VAR identification theory `doi:10.1111/j.1467-937x.2009.00578.x`, `doi:10.1257/aer.20151569`; Nakamura-Steinsson "Identification in macroeconomics" `doi:10.1257/jep.32.3.59` (library; not read in full this session since the AEA PDF returned 403).
4. **What is NOT identified without more assumptions: arbitrary policy-rule counterfactuals.** Lucas critique `doi:10.1016/s0167-2231(76)80003-6`. McKay-Wolf `doi:10.3982/ecta21045` (abs): in a general family of linearised structural models, empirically estimable causal effects of contemporaneous *and news* shocks to the policy rule suffice to build counterfactuals under alternative rules, robust to the Lucas critique, but only within that model family and with limited-evidence strategies needed in practice. `arXiv:2409.09577` (abs): LP-identified policy-peg/path counterfactuals coincide with VAR/structural counterfactuals *under policy invariance and a linear MA envelope*. `arXiv:2603.20394` (abs): the "potential system" gives nonparametric content to when time-series predictions are causal. DML for time series `doi:10.48550/arxiv.2603.10999`, semiparametric LPs `doi:10.48550/arxiv.2606.13519` (abs) are the 2026 ML-meets-identification entries.
5. **Firm-level counterfactuals.** Synthetic control `doi:10.3386/t0335`, synthetic DiD `arXiv:1812.09970`, matrix completion for panels `arXiv:1710.10251`, BSTS causal impact `arXiv:1506.00356` identify effects of *discrete, observed interventions* on treated units under parallel-trend/low-rank assumptions; they do not identify "what if revenue drivers changed" in the absence of an exogenous intervention. Counterfactual RNN-type methods `arXiv:2002.04083` (CRN), `arXiv:1902.00450` (Time Series Deconfounder), `arXiv:2204.07258` (Causal Transformer) require sequential ignorability or deconfounding assumptions that are untestable and implausible for firm decisions (assistant judgement, high confidence that the assumption is unverifiable).
6. **Consequence for an action-conditioned JEPA.** The library contains a theory result that action-conditioning alone may not give causal state (`arXiv:2609.31161`, other facet). The finance literature adds: actions (rate decisions) are endogenous, rare (on the order of 8 FOMC meetings/year), and best handled by conditioning on *identified shock series* (HFI instruments) rather than on raw policy rates. A defensible claim is "conditional forecast given an identified shock," validated against LP/VAR impulse responses and the Proxy-DFM benchmark; a claim about "what if the policy rate were X" must be restricted to the model family and invariance assumptions above, and disclosed as such. Confidence: high on the econometric logic, medium on feasibility.

LLM-as-simulator papers (contrast only): StockAgent-style and LLM-agent markets `arXiv:2406.19966`, `arXiv:2504.10789`; system-level risk from LLM agents `arXiv:2609.04373` (all abs). They need no data but have no validated counterfactual semantics.

Data sources (web, accessed 2026-10-01): FactSet Revere: customer/supplier/partner/competitor relationships extracted from 10-Ks, presentations and press releases, 150,000+ relationships, history from 2003, 40,000+ listed firms (https://fact-set.com/marketplace/catalog/product/factset-supply-chain-relationships; https://www.library.hbs.edu/databases-cases-and-more/datasets/factset-revere-supply-chain-relationships). Compustat Segments: only customers >10% of sales are disclosed (SFAS 131); documented problems: large-cap tilt, missing links, name inconsistencies, pre-1997 abbreviations (via https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~afrazzin/pdf/Economic%20Links%20and%20Predictable%20Returns%20-%20Cohen%20and%20Frazzini.pdf and secondary web summary; verify against primary documentation). Both sources are static, sparse and large-firm-biased; transaction-level data (customs, payments) are proprietary.

## 8. Which use case is most tractable under data scarcity (assistant judgement, evidence cited)
| Criterion | (a) Macro scenarios | (b) Firm fundamentals nowcast/imputation | (c) Supply chain |
|---|---|---|---|
| Public data, point-in-time | FRED/ALFRED vintages, ~800 monthly obs | Compustat/XBRL/WRDS; thousands of firms x ~40 quarters | sparse links, large-firm bias; transaction data proprietary |
| Ground truth for evaluation | none for scenarios; yes for conditional forecasts and IRFs | **yes: mask disclosed values; held-out quarters** | yes for link prediction; propagation only via natural experiments |
| Strong baselines to beat | DFM/BVAR/GDPNow, growth-at-risk | cross-sectional mean (`arXiv:2207.13071`), BLLP imputer (`doi:10.1093/rfs/hhae036`), TabPFN/TabICL (`arXiv:2605.10896`) | link-prediction GNNs, I-O models |
| Causal burden | high (rate what-ifs need identification) | low if scoped to nowcasting; counterfactual firm what-ifs not identifiable | high for shock propagation |
| Fit to JEPA | latent macro state; small n hurts | masked-field/cross-time prediction in a firm x time x variable tensor is a natural JEPA task; honest risk: signal may be weak (blockwise missingness) | graph-JEPA possible but needs data |
Recommendation (confidence medium): wedge on (b) as a *state inference with partial observation* problem evaluated by masking; add (a) as a second stage using identified HFI shocks as actions and Proxy-DFM/LP responses as validation targets; treat (c) as an extension where Compustat/Revere graph context is a feature, not the headline. Counter-evidence to monitor: Chen's result (`arXiv:2207.13071`) implies the observed data may contain little information about missing values, which would cap any imputer, JEPA included; test this first with a cheap pilot (mean imputer vs BLLP vs TabPFN on masked Compustat fields).
