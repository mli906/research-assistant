# Missing Values Handling for Machine Learning Portfolios (A. Y. Chen) - arXiv:2207.13071

Read: abstract (from library record) only; a full-text fetch through WebFetch returned a summary that contradicted the abstract on the main finding, so it was discarded. Do not rely on it beyond the abstract. Accessed 2026-10-01.

**Abstract claims.** 159 cross-sectional predictors; simply imputing with cross-sectional means performs well compared with rigorous EM imputation, because (1) missingness comes in large time-blocks, (2) cross-sectional correlations among predictors are small, (3) missingness is organised by underlying data source; hence observed data carry little information about missing values, and sophisticated imputation adds estimation noise that can underperform.
**Why it matters.** It is the counterweight to Bryzgalova, Lerner, Lettau, Pelger (doi:10.1093/rfs/hhae036, abstract: >70% of firms, ~half of market cap affected; imputation exploiting time-series and cross-sectional dependence changes risk-premium estimates). Together they say: imputation gains depend on whether structure (low-rank factors, persistence) is exploitable, a testable condition for a latent-state model on firm panels. Any JEPA/nowcasting claim on firm fundamentals must beat cross-sectional-mean and the BLLP (XP-Value-style) imputer, not zero-fill.

## Update 2026-10-01 (gap analysis): FULL TEXT read (arXiv PDF v6, Jan 2024, via pypdf; authors Chen and McCoy)
- **Scope of the ceiling.** It covers 159 *cross-sectional return predictors* (CZ dataset, monthly, standardised). The evaluation is long-short portfolio returns. Imputation accuracy of raw fundamentals (revenue, cash flow, headcount) is **not** the target.
- **Mechanism (verbatim):** "For common stocks on major exchanges that are missing book-to-market this month, 81% are also missing book-to-market in every previous month. As a result, there is little time-series information about missing values." Also: "For stocks that are missing book-to-market, about 90% are also missing earnings-to-price." "the first 10 principal components span only 40% of total variance."
- **Their own masking test is admittedly optimistic (verbatim):** "the RMSEs in Figure 4 are surely understated, as our random masking does not account for the fact that accounting predictors are typically missing together ... the imputation exercise in Figure 4 can 'cheat'". Mask 10% at random → EM RMSE ≥0.70 for below-median-cap stocks, against 1.00 for mean imputation on standardised data.
- **Baselines included:** cross-sectional mean, EM, industry-size decile means, and "Last Observed" (last value within 12 months).
- **Implication.** The ceiling is strongest for *never-observed* blocks (entity-level or source-level missingness), which is the private-firm analogue. It says little about *ragged-edge* nowcasting of a firm's not-yet-filed quarter, where the firm's own history exists. A fair benchmark needs **source-structured masks** (all of Compustat for a firm-quarter, or a whole statement), not random cells, and must keep Last-Observed and mean as baselines.
