# arXiv:2608.20516 When Graph-JEPA Learns the Wrong Thing: Diagnosing and Repairing Category-Conditional Collapse
Read: HTML full text via WebFetch extraction (2026-10-01).
- **Failure:** a Graph-JEPA (predict a masked aspect from a subgraph's other aspects; corpus of 57,903 articles) shows linear-probe accuracy 0.871 and effective rank 18-47 of 128 (both "healthy") while retrieval recovers 0.00 of 14.379 recoverable bits (MRR 1.9e-4 vs chance 1.99e-4). 99.61% of embedding variance is allocated to aspect identity, instance identity 0.39%.
- **Cause:** coupled predictor + EMA-target objective admits a degenerate minimum: if the target designator determines aspect identity, any function of aspect identity gets zero loss.
- **Repair:** remove per-aspect mean from the target + in-batch negatives: 14.377/14.379 bits; switching loss from InfoNCE to regression reverses a 14.05-bit gain (the objective is the lever). Second failure: the repaired retrieval target is structurally reducible (parameter-free oracle reaches 96.4% of ceiling).
- **Recommendations:** reducibility audits, variance-allocation decomposition, parameter-free baselines and positive controls, pre-registered data-quality gates, never rely on a single probe/rank metric.
- **Relevance (high for evaluation design):** effective rank and linear probes do not detect "collapse into the context category". In a financial panel, a latent could encode sector/period identity (the masked-view designator) and look healthy. Add instance-level retrieval and shuffled/parameter-free controls to the pre-registered diagnostics. Graph-domain, single corpus; generalisation to finance is inference (M).
